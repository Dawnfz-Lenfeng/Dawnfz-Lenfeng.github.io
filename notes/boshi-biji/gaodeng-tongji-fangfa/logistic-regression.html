1.6 Study Design: Prospective and Case-Control

Definition (Prospective and Retrospective Sampling)

In a prospective study, subjects are sampled by exposure or covariates, and outcomes are observed subsequently. In a retrospective case-control study, subjects are sampled by outcome status, and their exposures or covariates are compared. Prospective studies can be expensive and inefficient for rare outcomes; case-control studies are often cheaper and more efficient for rare diseases. In a case-control study, the proportion of cases is determined by the sampling design and generally does not estimate the population prevalence.

3.4 Wald versus Likelihood Ratio

Note (Comparing the two tests)

  • Both tests are large-sample approximations.
  • The likelihood ratio test can handle one restriction or several restrictions simultaneously. It is invariant to reparameterization and is not one-sided.
  • The Wald test is convenient for a single coefficient or a linear combination of coefficients, and it requires fitting only the full model.
  • For a single , the two tests can lead to different conclusions because both rely on approximations. This differs from linear regression, where the and tests are exact under normality.

5.4 Residual Analysis and Diagnostics

Note (Using residuals)

  • The exact distribution of residuals under the true model is unknown, so residual plots are interpreted qualitatively.
  • Plot residuals against fitted values or covariates. A flat lowess smooth suggests that the systematic part of the model is adequate.
  • Pearson and deviance residuals are often similar and can be checked with a QQ plot for outliers. They can differ substantially when the data are sparse, such as individual Bernoulli data.
  • Working residuals are useful for checking nonlinearity against covariates.
  • At the MLE, logistic regression can be written as a weighted least-squares problem with working responseand weights . Delete-one diagnostics such as DFBETAS and Cook's distance can therefore be computed from this weighted least-squares representation.

Key distinctions:

  • The Wald test is convenient for a single coefficient or a linear combination; the likelihood ratio test compares nested models and handles several restrictions jointly.
  • Pearson's and the deviance both compare the fitted model with the saturated model. Their approximations require replicated data with adequate expected counts.
  • For individual Bernoulli data, and can be computed but should not be referred to a distribution.
  • The Hosmer–Lemeshow test groups subjects by fitted probability; it is a useful diagnostic but is not an omnibus test of model correctness.
